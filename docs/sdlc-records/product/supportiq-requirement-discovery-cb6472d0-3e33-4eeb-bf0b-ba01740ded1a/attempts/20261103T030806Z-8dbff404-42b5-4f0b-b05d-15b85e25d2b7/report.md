# Requirement Analysis

## 1. Understanding

- **Authoritative input analyzed:** `docs/requirement/SupportIQ_Product_Requirements_Document.docx`, PRD v1.0, September 2026, SHA-256 `14AF65ACC24885C2378D47313EF41C6B0573283602913F812B7D9E36746709B5`. It is explicitly draft context, not an approved Requirement.
- **Business objective:** A learning/portfolio SupportIQ portal for the fictional CloudDesk B2B SaaS scenario. It is intended to help support staff find approved knowledge and handle tickets through tenant-aware retrieval, assistive AI, ticket intelligence, read-only tools, and quality/operations capabilities. The stated goal is to reduce search/composition time, but the PRD says impact is to be measured in a later pilot rather than assumed.
- **Actors:** Tenant Admin, Support Agent, Support Manager, Platform Admin, application/service identity, and human knowledge reviewers. External ticket/customer systems are mentioned but not identified.
- **Main capability units:** local app identity and tenant authorization; knowledge ingestion/lifecycle and RAG; ticket summary/classification; human-reviewed reply drafting; constrained read-only tool use; basic analytics; AI evaluation/testing; security and AI operations; deployment/CI/telemetry.
- **Nature of the work:** This is full draft requirement discovery and scope establishment, not a selected implementation feature or one implementation task. The source defines an intended first demonstrable release, plus deferred items and implementation guidance.
- **Source status:** The Document Control table says “Draft updated with approved decisions” and also says it is subject to Requirement Analysis refresh, complete Verification, and Human Requirement Approval. The PRD’s “Decision” rows are treated here as explicit statements in the draft; their separate approval provenance is not independently established by this input.
- **Decision provenance:** The supplied PB-01/PB-02 are assessment possible-blocker dispositions only. They are not new product requirements, Requirement approval, Plan approval, a waiver, or acceptance. Their original decision wording and rationale remain in the linked portability decision-handoff evidence.

## 2. Explicit Requirements

Source locators use the PRD section headings and requirement IDs; table row references below refer to the document tables.

### Product purpose, users, and scope

- **§§1–2:** Learning/portfolio, production-minded support portal; grounded answers with traceable sources and abstention; tests for retrieval/quality/citations/structured output/tenant isolation/APIs; demonstrable application with setup, CI, monitoring, and cost controls. Business benefit is to be measured, not presumed. Dataset counts in §2 are initial targets, expressly not hard minimums.
- **§3:** Tenant Admin manages own-tenant settings/users/knowledge; Support Agent accesses approved/current knowledge and authorized/assigned own-tenant tickets and prepares drafts for review; Support Manager manages/reviews tickets, team feedback, and own-tenant category/priority mapping; Platform Admin operates service health/configuration, with no routine tenant-content access and exceptional cross-tenant access only if time-limited, reasoned, and audited. App users authenticate through SQL-backed local SupportIQ accounts, not end-user SQL credentials. Passwords are salted-hash stored; account creation is restricted; sign-in attempts are rate-limited; a separate protected least-privilege app database identity is used. API-level least-privilege tenant roles are explicit/testable; demo roles may be seeded.
- **§4.1 (first demonstrable release):** Local app accounts and tenant authorization; knowledge upload through deletion; RAG chat, context, citations, abstention and feedback; ticket list/detail with AI classification/summary; human-reviewed copilot; read-only customer/ticket integration tools with auditable execution and no integrated business-data writes or message sending; tests, GitHub workflow, CI, telemetry, Azure deployment.
- **§4.2 (deferred/optional):** Advanced natural-language analytics/arbitrary text-to-SQL; complex autonomous multi-agent or unsupervised communication; production external ticketing/CRM/email/messaging integrations; enterprise SSO, billing, multi-region DR, compliance certifications; large-scale benchmarking, custom fine-tuning, automated model selection.

### Functional requirement units (§5)

- **AI Customer Assistant, FR-CA-01–08:** Start conversations and ask natural-language questions; retrieve only approved/current knowledge authorized for the current user; ground answers and show source title plus excerpt/section where available; abstain and flag for human review when evidence is insufficient, stale, or conflicting, without silently choosing; optionally use recent turns without crossing tenant/access boundaries; positive/negative feedback with optional reason; role-limited history; English initially with later-language extensibility.
- **Knowledge Base, FR-KB-01–08:** Accept PDF/DOCX/TXT, maximum 25 MiB per file; clearly reject unsupported/oversized files; validate extension, content type, size, and processing outcome without trusting filename alone; purge failed/rejected uploads and derived content within 24 hours; store originals and tenant/uploader/time/version/status metadata; retain approved originals while current until superseded/deleted; extract/normalize, chunk, embed, index; expose processing states and a versioned review/approval lifecycle; updates/reprocessing produce new versions and require review/approval by someone other than submitter; re-review approved/current knowledge at 180 days; deletion immediately blocks retrieval and purges originals and derived content within 24 hours; encrypted backups age out within 30 days; retain only content-free deletion/audit metadata for 365 days; only approved/current versions are searchable/citable; stale/conflicting sources trigger abstention and human flagging.
- **AI Ticket Management, FR-TK-01–07:** Ticket list/detail use deterministic synthetic seed data; concise summaries; structured classification to tenant-configurable categories/priorities whose mapping is owned by that tenant’s Support Manager; sentiment/urgency are suggestions, not facts; supported rule-based queue/escalation suggestions only and no autonomous escalation; agents can edit/override suggestions for the in-app synthetic demo, retaining the final human-approved value and, where appropriate, the original suggestion; no writeback to integrated systems; invalid model output must not corrupt ticket data.
- **Agent Copilot, FR-AC-01–07:** Generate a suggested reply for a selected ticket; recommend sourced knowledge; summarize authorized available customer records; suggest next steps only when supported by approved knowledge; allow edit/copy/discard/regenerate; permit editable customer-facing drafts for human review but no message sending; distinguish internal notes from customer-facing draft content.
- **Workflow Automation, FR-WF-01–06:** Small allowlist of read-only tools (examples: knowledge search, ticket/customer lookup); typed I/O, authorization, timeout, and error handling; prohibit model-driven arbitrary SQL, shell, or unrestricted network requests; first release has no integration business-data write actions; auditable tool call/outcome/actor/tenant/correlation IDs; communicate failures without secrets or stack traces.
- **Support Analytics, FR-AN-01–05:** Basic authorized-data ticket counts by status/category/priority; simple trends and SLA indicators only when sample fields exist; small set of natural-language questions through predefined allowlisted analytics functions; identify data scope/time range; arbitrary text-to-SQL out of scope, with any later exploration constrained to read-only, validated, row-limited, tenant-scoped access.
- **Evaluation/Testing, FR-EV-01–06:** Versioned evaluation set with expected sources/answer characteristics; record retrieval, answer, citation, latency, model/deployment, prompt version; assess relevance, grounding, citation correctness, abstention; compare changes with baseline; test ambiguous, missing/conflicting knowledge, prompt injection, and cross-tenant access; results are decision support, not proof; document limits and thresholds; a Human must approve numeric quality thresholds before release, and no numeric values are set.
- **Security/AI Operations, FR-OP-01–08:** Tenant association and enforcement on every read/write path; API and relevant UI role access; managed secret store/no committed secrets; errors/latency/model usage where available/correlation IDs; minimize sensitive log content and make retention/redaction configurable; request/rate limits, timeouts, graceful model/search failure; approximate AI usage/cost per feature/tenant when provider metadata allows; repeatable CI/CD deployment/configuration.

### Workflows, architecture, data, interfaces, quality, and delivery

- **§6:** Login/workspace → identity/role/tenant/limits/context validation → authorized approved/current retrieval → evidence-grounded model → answer/citations or no-answer → feedback. Ingestion validates upload, records tenant-scoped version, asynchronously extracts/chunks/embeds/indexes, reports safe status, and keeps superseded versions non-searchable. Ticket flow uses authorized context, labels outputs AI-generated, and leaves editable draft review with a person.
- **§7:** React/TypeScript, .NET 8 Web API, Azure AI model endpoint, Azure AI Search, Blob Storage, Azure SQL, worker/Azure Functions, Key Vault, Application Insights, and GitHub Actions are identified. Model/deployment/region selection is deferred until availability, quota, cost, and embedding/index compatibility are validated. API mediates model/search; browser receives no provider keys/unrestricted credentials; local/Azure configuration abstractions are required.
- **§8:** Tenant, User/Membership, KnowledgeDocument/Chunk, Conversation/ChatMessage, Citation, Ticket, TicketAIResult, Feedback, ToolAudit/EvaluationRun are illustrative logical entities only—not a final schema. Indexes, foreign keys, retention, and tenant-aware uniqueness are deferred to implementation.
- **§9:** Example chat/history/knowledge/lifecycle/ticket/ticket-AI/draft/feedback/health API contracts are illustrative. Final contracts are to define schemas, validation, status/error responses, idempotency where needed, and OpenAPI documentation.
- **§10–11:** Security/privacy, synthetic data, deletion/retention, failure/retry, pagination/asynchronous ingestion, latency baselines before guarantees, loose coupling, accessibility, observability, usage caps/budget/teardown, and provider abstraction are stated. AI must ground/cite without fabricated citations, abstain/flag stale or conflicting evidence, treat source materials as untrusted input, prevent cross-tenant access, constrain tools, require human review for drafts/business-changing actions, validate structured output, version prompts/evaluations, and label classifications/sentiment provisional/editable.
- **§12:** Fabricated names/content only; no real customer records or secrets in prompts/logs/fixtures/screenshots. Dataset sizes are initial targets, not minimums: 20 knowledge articles, 100 synthetic tickets, 5 fictional organizations, 10 support agents, 50 question/source pairs, 10 no-answer questions, and 10 prompt-injection cases.
- **§13–14:** Acceptance criteria include safe upload/failed purge; only approved/current indexed content retrievable; immediate retrieval block and FR-KB-07 deletion; direct-API tenant isolation across documents/chunks/tickets/conversations/feedback; documented schema and safe invalid-output handling; editable/discardable drafts with no sends or integration writes; read-only authorized bounded/audited tool calls; PR CI tests/build with failures blocking normal merge; documented setup/deployment and no repository secrets; evaluation run with model/prompt identifiers and Human-approved numeric quality thresholds before release. Test levels include unit, integration, API, AI evaluation, end-to-end, and operational smoke tests.
- **§15–16:** A suggested GitHub/Copilot issue → plan → small implementation → tests → PR/CI/human review workflow is provided. A suggested 30-day week-by-week delivery boundary ends in a deployed demo, but the PRD explicitly says this is not a production-readiness commitment; a thin vertical slice is preferred over optional features.
- **§17–18 and appendices:** CloudDesk fiction/synthetic data, model risk, tenant leakage, parsing/indexing failure, and cost risk are called out. Azure AI stays in baseline while model/deployment/region remain undecided. The section labels authentication, lifecycle, ticket mapping/synthetic seed, and read-only integration scope as “Decision”; numeric evaluation thresholds remain open. Definition of Done, initial backlog, and glossary provide delivery examples and terms, not additional approved functionality.

## 3. Scope

### In Scope

The draft’s stated first demonstrable release capabilities in §4.1, subject to the unresolved boundaries below: tenant-aware local accounts and authorization; knowledge lifecycle and grounded RAG; synthetic demo tickets with AI summaries/classification; human-reviewed reply drafting; narrowly bounded read-only tools; tests/evaluation; CI, telemetry, cost controls, and an Azure demo deployment.

### Out of Scope

Per §4.2 and explicit functional limits: arbitrary text-to-SQL, autonomous multi-agent/unsupervised customer communication, production external ticketing/CRM/email/messaging integrations, enterprise SSO, billing, multi-region DR, compliance certification, large-scale benchmarking, custom fine-tuning, automatic model selection, integration writes, and sending customer messages. No product implementation, source change, test, build, deployment, or live integration was performed in this Analysis.

### Deferred / Undecided

- Exact Azure AI model/deployment/region; validate availability, quota, cost, and embedding/index compatibility first.
- Whether any first-release integration connects to a real external system or only exposes local/synthetic data through read-only tools.
- Final API schemas, status/error/idempotency contracts, data schema, and worker/provider choices where alternatives remain.
- Lifecycle state transitions, reviewer authority, stale/conflict criteria, policy for missed 180-day review, and final retention behavior for superseded/expired/rejected versions.
- Numeric evaluation quality thresholds; a Human approval before release is explicit, values are not.
- Exact role permissions and exceptional Platform Admin access approval/recording criteria.
- Numeric performance/availability/service-level targets; the draft requests baselines before guarantees.
- Whether the suggested 30-day sequence is a binding schedule or guidance; the text labels the delivery boundary “Suggested.”
- Which draft §17 “Decision” rows have separate approval provenance; the source itself remains a draft.

## 8. Technical Impact

This section identifies implications of the source; it does not prescribe an implementation or create requirements.

- **Identity/API/domain:** Local account lifecycle, tenant membership, role permission map, service identity, and exception auditing affect every API, data model, UI action, and direct-API test. Privileged cross-tenant access needs a fail-closed, auditable contract before that boundary is implemented.
- **Knowledge/data pipeline:** File validation, safe storage, asynchronous extraction/chunking/embedding/indexing, versioning, independent review, lifecycle state, re-review, deletion, backup aging, and audit retention span upload APIs, worker state, blob/search/database stores, retries, and user-visible status. Immediate retrieval blocking and eventual purge are separate behaviors.
- **RAG/model/UI:** Approved/current-only tenant-scoped retrieval, evidence quality, citations, untrusted-content handling, no-answer behavior, conversation context, prompt/version tracking, and review flags affect request contracts, model inputs/outputs, user-visible citations, logging, and evaluation.
- **Ticket/copilot/tools:** Synthetic seed data, tenant-defined mappings, typed outputs, editable AI suggestions, read-only tools, no send/writeback, and auditable tool calls affect ticket contracts, action boundaries, role checks, audit metadata, and UI distinctions between internal and customer-facing text.
- **Analytics/evaluation:** Definitions for data scope/time range/SLA, evaluation datasets, baseline comparisons, metric thresholds, and model/prompt identifiers affect reproducibility and release evidence. No threshold or SLA value is derivable from this draft.
- **Platform/dependencies:** The named React/.NET/Azure baseline depends on Azure AI availability/quota/cost/embedding-index compatibility and storage/search/database/secret/telemetry services. Worker choice and exact model/deployment/region remain open. The browser must not receive provider credentials.
- **Quality/operations:** Unit/integration/API/AI/e2e/operational tests, CI, telemetry, throttling/timeouts/retries, configuration, deployment, cost controls, teardown, accessibility, and synthetic-only data are all in the draft. No test/build/runtime/deployment or live integration was executed in this Analysis.

### Technical recommendations (not requirements)

1. Before finalizing the Requirement, express knowledge approval and processing status as a traceable state/transition contract and separately define retrieval eligibility.
2. Keep any selected first-release connector behind the source-approved read-only boundary, with explicit tenant authorization, typed operation contracts, timeout/error behavior, and audit coverage.
3. Keep all unapproved thresholds, service-level values, provider/model/region choices, API schemas, and retention-policy extensions as explicit decisions rather than defaults.
4. Map each requirement unit to later plan tasks and tests only after Human decisions and Requirement approval; this Analysis does not create that Plan.

## 9. Questions Requiring Human Decision

These are questions for the parent to present. The Agent does not select an option or update the PRD. Options are illustrative choices, not recommendations or approved requirements.

1. **First-release integration boundary (RA-A01 / RA-C01 / RA-M01):** Should first-release read-only lookup use (A) synthetic/local demo data only, with all real external connectors deferred; (B) one explicitly named external read-only connector; or (C) another bounded source? Which lookup operations/data are allowed? This determines secrets/access, privacy, failure behavior, test setup, and whether §4.2’s production integration deferral is preserved.
2. **Knowledge lifecycle and authority (RA-A02 / RA-M02):** Which roles may submit, review/approve, reprocess, expire, and delete knowledge? Should processing status be separate from approval status, and what transitions are allowed? What should happen when 180-day re-review is overdue (e.g. remain searchable, become unsearchable pending review, or another explicit rule)? Who may approve besides the submitter?
3. **Stale/conflicting content and review routing (RA-A03 / RA-M03):** What makes knowledge stale or conflicting beyond the stated 180-day review cadence? Who receives the human-review flag, and what state/retrieval behavior applies while it is unresolved? Should overdue content automatically cease to be searchable, or only when a human marks it stale?
4. **Account and privileged access (RA-A04 / RA-M04):** What account lifecycle operations are required (creation, disablement, recovery)? Which permissions belong to each role? For exceptional Platform Admin cross-tenant access, who authorizes it, what maximum duration/reason/audit fields are required, and how is access revoked? The source requires time-limited, reasoned, audited access but does not choose these controls.
5. **Deletion and retention boundaries (RA-A05 / RA-M05):** Is deletion per version or for the whole logical document? When does the 24-hour purge clock start, and how should a failed purge be handled? What happens to superseded/expired/rejected originals and derived content? Please confirm the stated 30-day encrypted-backup age-out and 365-day content-free audit retention remain distinct.
6. **Ticket taxonomy and human review (RA-A06):** What initial tenant category/priority values/defaults and mapping-change authority are required? Which supported rules may suggest a queue/escalation? Which human-approved override value and original suggestion must be retained, and for how long? The AI must not autonomously escalate.
7. **Analytics and SLA meaning (RA-A07):** Which data fields, time period/calendar/timezone, and SLA definition should the demo use? Are indicators illustrative only when sample fields exist, or must they represent a defined service policy?
8. **Evaluation/release thresholds (RA-M08):** Which metrics and aggregation should be used and who approves numeric quality thresholds before release? The PRD provides no values. Consistent with PB-02, those thresholds and applicable evidence must exist before affected runtime/integration/release boundaries; they are not blockers to this initial Analysis.
9. **Content privacy and history (RA-A09 / RA-A11 / RA-M09):** What retention/access rules apply to conversations, feedback, ticket/customer context, prompts, and internal notes? Which content may enter model context or logs, and what retention/redaction settings are expected? Synthetic-only demo data remains the explicit source boundary.
10. **Draft status and delivery intent (RA-A10 / RA-A12 / RA-M11):** Which §17 entries marked “Decision” have separate Human decision provenance, if that provenance is intended to be authoritative? Is the “30-day build” a binding delivery schedule or a learning goal? The suggested boundary and non-production-readiness statement are not a fixed release commitment.

Routine technical contract details (exact API names/schemas/status codes, entity schema, provider-specific worker shape, and performance baselines) may be refined in governed planning once business scope and applicable engineering constraints are clear; do not use that route to decide the business questions above.
